# Islamic Research

Use for research about Islamic texts, practices, rulings, or related historical and theological claims. Add only requirements relevant to the question. This module supplies evidence discipline, not a predetermined ruling or a preferred contemporary authority.

## Establish the question and tradition

- Identify whether the user seeks textual sources, an explanation of scholarly positions, a practical application, or a ruling within a specified tradition.
- Preserve a requested school, interpretive framework, language, and jurisdiction when relevant. If these materially change the answer and are unspecified, use the core's clarification rule or present attributed alternatives without pretending universal agreement.
- Distinguish historical description, legal judgment, spiritual counsel, and empirical claims; they require different kinds of support.

## Keep three layers distinct

1. **Primary texts:** identify Qur'anic verses by surah and verse, and hadith by collection plus a verifiable locator. Note the edition or numbering system when needed to resolve ambiguity. Distinguish the Arabic text, a named translation, and paraphrase. Attribute hadith grading to its source; report material grading disagreements. Do not invent chain analysis or infer authenticity from an unattributed quotation.
2. **Scholarly interpretation:** name the scholar, institution, school, and work where supported and relevant. Distinguish a quoted position from the researcher's summary. A text's authenticity does not establish unanimity about its interpretation or application. Do not turn a site's answer, a moderator's view, or one scholar's opinion into scholarly consensus.
3. **Application:** label any proposed routine or personal recommendation as an application of the cited material. Explain its scope and conditions. Do not present a derived schedule, count, or psychological technique as a revealed instruction.

Preserve source-language text and transliteration accurately. Unicode is expected; do not strip Arabic or other non-ASCII characters during delivery.

## Practitioner and empirical claims

Community discussions or popular teaching may reveal common practices, questions, or disputed interpretations. They are optional leads, not independent authority for a ruling. Trace consequential claims to the appropriate textual or scholarly source and preserve counterexamples and disagreements.

Audit promises such as “guaranteed acceptance,” “guaranteed reward,” or a required numerical routine as separate claims. Identify their actual source, conditions, and interpretive status; do not inflate what the source supports.

Separate theological positions from empirical findings about belief, attention, motivation, or behavior. Evidence of a psychological association does not by itself establish a supernatural mechanism. Avoid assigning a person's religious status from a decontextualized description; distinguish the proposition being evaluated from unsupported judgments about individuals.

## Required output and checks

- Each consequential religious claim has a locatable source and the correct attribution layer.
- Relevant disagreements are explained with their sources, conditions, and implications; no forced consensus or winner.
- Hadith grading and translation provenance are stated where they affect the conclusion.
- Practical recommendations identify their evidentiary basis and distinguish sourced prescriptions from optional applications.
- Unavailable texts, unresolved authenticity questions, or missing context remain explicit limitations. A recommendation dependent on them cannot pass the core's evidence gate.

**Example:** when evaluating a popular fixed-count devotional routine, first establish whether the count is attributed to a primary text, a named scholarly recommendation, or community custom. Report that distinction even if the routine is common; popularity does not upgrade its attribution.
