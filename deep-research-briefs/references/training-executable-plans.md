# Training and Executable Plans

Use for individualized training research and other reports that produce an executable schedule, protocol, budget, or adjustment plan. Apply domain-specific checks only where relevant; do not import exercise requirements into unrelated plans.

## Intake and baseline

Establish the variables that can materially change the prescription before making it individualized. For training, these commonly include the goal, experience, current program, recent progression, available time and equipment, recovery constraints, preferences, and relevant pain or injury limitations. For another domain, identify its equivalent baseline, resources, constraints, and risk signals.

Reuse supplied intake and previous research after checking relevance and freshness. Ask once for missing material inputs using the core's intake rule. If the user wants the brief first, make the missing intake an explicit execution prerequisite; do not fill it with invented personal data or imply the plan is individualized.

## Evidence and synthesis

- Match the research population, intervention, outcome, timeframe, and measurement method to the intended use. State where transfer to the user is uncertain.
- Distinguish measured results from mechanistic hypotheses, practitioner experience, and forecasts. Coaching practice may generate useful leads but does not establish causal efficacy or safety by popularity.
- Compare against the current practice or a feasible simpler alternative. Include a no-change option where warranted.
- Prefer a practical conditional recommendation over a precise forecast that the evidence cannot support. Do not invent expected individual gains from group averages.
- Resolve conflicting recommendations by their relevant conditions rather than averaging incompatible prescriptions.

## One canonical plan

Keep one authoritative prescription. The narrative, schedules, phase maps, workload totals, fallback options, and summary must reconcile with it. Mark optional variants clearly and specify when each replaces the default.

For each material action or adjustment rule, specify:

- the action, dose or amount, frequency, and duration where applicable;
- the starting condition and the observable signal that triggers a change;
- the observation window and the concrete next action;
- relevant limits, fallback, or stopping conditions supported by the evidence.

Use observations the intended user can actually obtain. If a laboratory or inaccessible measurement is essential, identify that prerequisite and offer an evidence-supported practical alternative when available.

## Audit and acceptance

- Recompute decision-driving arithmetic with tools: units, totals, rates, phase lengths, budgets, and any derived quantities. A plausible number is not a verified calculation.
- Trace each load-bearing prescription to appropriate evidence or an explicitly labeled inference, and test whether the inference is warranted.
- Reconcile every representation of the plan, including failure and fallback cases.
- Check that adjustment rules can be executed without guessing; distinguish an observation threshold from a guaranteed forecast.
- Identify unsupported personalization, unresolved high-impact conflicts, or unavailable prerequisites. Apply the core's unapproved disposition until repaired.

**Example:** “Increase volume when recovery is good” is incomplete. A supported rule must define the observations and observation window, identify which part of the canonical plan changes, and state the fallback when those conditions are absent. Do not invent universal threshold values to complete the format.
