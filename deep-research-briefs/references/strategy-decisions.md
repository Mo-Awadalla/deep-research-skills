# Strategy Decisions

Use when research informs a choice among strategies, products, builds, applications, or competition entries. The goal is a decision justified by evidence and constraints, not a compulsory winner.

## Establish the decision

Read the current primary artifact where one exists: the event rules, RFP, job posting, procurement criteria, or other authoritative brief. Record the source and retrieval date for material constraints, deadlines, eligibility, deliverables, and published evaluation criteria. Report discrepancies with secondary descriptions and verify which source governs instead of silently averaging them.

Keep the author's preliminary option set revisable. Give candidates stable identifiers and state the inclusion and exclusion criteria; do not claim the full option space is known. Research may add, merge, eliminate, or split candidates. Record material changes and evaluate the resulting viable set consistently.

## Compare fairly

- Prefer published evaluation criteria and actual constraints. Distinguish a hard feasibility gate from a scored preference.
- Use a comparison method suited to the decision. Define scales and units before applying numerical scores; do not add numbers merely to make uncertain judgments look precise.
- Where weights are needed, show their source. User priorities are inputs; inferred weights are assumptions. Assess sensitivity to plausible alternatives.
- Do not derive confident personal preferences or one scoring column per evaluator merely from public biographies. A named evaluator matters only when documented relevant criteria or actions justify that analysis; label any remaining inference.
- Compare important candidates with relevant prior work or deployed alternatives. Describe the actual difference, including when an existing solution is preferable to building one.
- Investigate crowding, adoption, or competitors' likely choices only when it materially affects the decision. Label forecasts, evidence gaps, and dependence on assumptions.
- Request a prototype or demonstration only when it can resolve a material uncertainty and is feasible within the authorized scope, time, tools, and permissions. A published adversarial payload is not a universal requirement.

## Decision and uncertainty

A valid outcome may be a supported winner, a conditional winner, a tie, a shortlist pending discriminating evidence, or no feasible option. Explain the conditions that would change the result. Distinguish lack of evidence from demonstrated equivalence.

When a key uncertainty can be reduced, identify the smallest useful next observation or experiment, its cost or prerequisite, and how each plausible result would change the decision. Do not claim an unexecuted demo or experiment produced evidence.

## Acceptance checks

- Current governing constraints are sourced and reflected in feasibility judgments.
- Candidate inclusion, changes, and exclusions are traceable; research was allowed to reveal a better option.
- Comparisons use relevant consistent criteria, with uncertainty and sensitivity visible where material.
- The recommendation is supported by the comparison and remains conditional where the evidence requires it.
- No fabricated preference, mandatory novelty, or compulsory exactly-one-winner rule substitutes for evidence.

**Example:** if two feasible build options trade speed against an uncertain judging preference, give the conditional choice and identify what preference would reverse it. Do not force a numeric winner by inventing a judge's weight.
